# On-demand ideal-line solver

The width-only runtime, initially prototyped at Spa, Suzuka and Nürburgring, calculates its ideal line in a browser Web Worker. Results exist only in session memory. The original trace, rounded width, endpoints, timing and compact elevation data stay in the database; no ideal-line coordinates are saved for all 1,182 configured layouts. The UI name remains **Ideal line**.

## Method and relationship to TUM

This is an independent TypeScript implementation based on the iterative minimum-curvature quadratic-programming concept in [TUM's trajectory planning helpers](https://github.com/TUMFTM/trajectory_planning_helpers/blob/master/trajectory_planning_helpers/opt_min_curv.py) and its [iterative QP handler](https://github.com/TUMFTM/trajectory_planning_helpers/blob/master/trajectory_planning_helpers/iqp_handler.py). It is not a direct port, does not run upstream Python/quadprog, and does not implement TUM's full minimum-time/IPOPT optimal-control solver. The previous browser prototype's 160 local coordinate sweeps and disabled time refinement have been replaced.

The source-derived reference and output remain sampled at no more than 1.5 m. Lateral displacement is represented by cubic B-spline controls spaced at approximately 6 m. The optimizer linearizes curvature with respect to these controls and solves a coupled box-constrained QP for the whole route, rather than moving isolated vertices. It minimizes squared planar curvature weighted by fixed reference distance. Control updates have a 2 m trust region. Bounds reserve half the vehicle width and an extra 0.12 m; nonnegative spline weights keep interpolated offsets within their control bounds. Open routes pin endpoint controls to preserve their endpoints and approach headings. Closed routes use periodic controls and account for the seam in the quadratic system.

The QP uses ADMM with banded Cholesky solves and a low-rank periodic seam correction. Reported QP convergence requires a maximum primal residual below 0.001 m and a normalized dual residual below 0.00001. Nonlinear curvature iterations stop at relative objective improvement below 0.000001 or maximum control movement below 0.005 m, with an 80-iteration guard. Diagnostics retain actual residuals, objective history and whether either stopping limit was reached; a finite result is not automatically called converged. This is numerical convergence for this discretization, not a global optimality guarantee.

The second stage compares the curvature seed with feasible blends toward a distance-minimizing spline QP seed. It then optimizes smooth lateral patches at 100, 60 and 35 m scales around reference turns, with larger and smaller updates over up to three sweeps. Curvature alone can favour the outside of an entire hairpin, so it is not used as the final racing-line objective. Every feasible candidate is scored with the same dense speed-dependent physical model; no coarse curvature/time ranking may veto an apex improvement.

For performance, candidate windows extend 350 m beyond the affected patch. Window endpoints preserve the current feasible entry/exit velocities, padded geometry supplies the true 3 m curvature window, and a candidate is rejected if it cannot match its joins. Accepted candidates reduce the constructed feasible lap time. Each sweep ends with a precise whole-route speed solve; a sweep that worsens that whole-route time is rolled back. Final time is recomputed globally and cannot be worse than the feasible curvature seed. This is constrained local time optimization, not a proof of a globally minimum lap time or a port of TUM's full nonlinear vehicle solver.

The precise speed roots use safeguarded Newton iteration in this optimizer, with bisection retained as the independent reference/default for older authoring calls. Focused comparisons cover grades, corners, different grip/power parameters and fixed window endpoint speeds. Physical-window curvature uses streaming segment lookup, numerically identical to the prior binary-search interpolation.

## Vehicle and clearance assumptions

The existing illustrative dry GT3 preset is retained: width 2.05 m, mass 1,350 kg, 410 kW, 285 km/h speed cap, 1.55 g mechanical lateral grip plus 0.85 g aero contribution at 200 km/h, capped at 3 g. The existing power, drag, traction/braking ellipse and braking limits are unchanged. Saved estimated road elevation and Suzuka's separate bridge height profile are included in the time calculation. Banking, tyre load sensitivity, detailed suspension, kerb use and calibrated vehicle aero are absent. These parameters are assumptions, not measured vehicle performance.

The **0.1 m margin is from the car side to the estimated saved-width corridor**, not from the vehicle centre. A 4.6 m × 2.05 m rectangular footprint is checked along the path, including quarter-step intermediate positions, against the actual generated inner and outer edge segments in route order. This avoids matching Suzuka's bridge to its other visit. A small numerical buffer is retained. If yaw or tight curvature requires more room than the lateral bounds, all spline controls retreat smoothly together; individual points are never pushed toward the centre independently. Candidate time changes are checked locally and the accepted final path is checked globally. This sampled envelope check is not a continuous collision proof or centimetre-accurate road survey.

Constant average widths cannot reproduce actual local widening or asphalt asymmetry. Minimum clearance is a constraint, not a requirement to touch every apex. A smoother or faster path can stay farther from an edge. In particular, Karussell's banking makes a planar point-mass result an estimate.

## Validation and delivery

Focused tests include the analytic outer-radius solution on a closed circle (including its periodic seam), independent 6 m versus 3 m control-grid refinement on a chicane, sensitivity to power/grip, precise-time improvement and lateral-g limits. Real source-layout checks cover runtime construction, trace identity, open endpoints, constant road width, convergence, playback and Suzuka's retained vertical separation. All twelve layouts are checked separately. Browser checks use the built Tailscale preview and test the worker, map overlays, compact downloads, playback and phone layout.

Only the persistent Tailscale preview is updated. Its existing routes and preview-specific imagery key are retained. Production rollout and full-database/imagery validation are outside this change. No measured telemetry was available, so real-lap agreement remains unvalidated.

The initial three-venue prototype run on 2026-11-10 converged for all twelve layouts. A separate denser rectangle-perimeter check tested 15,808,320 perimeter points and found a minimum sampled side clearance of 0.11005 m. The 9 focused unit/runtime tests and 4 deployed-browser tests passed, along with TypeScript, ESLint and the Vite build. Cold worker calculations ranged from 1.1 to 28.2 seconds locally; browser layout-ready measurements were 3.2 seconds for Spa, 2.6 for Suzuka and 19.7 for Nordschleife BTG. These are machine-specific prototype timings. Subsequent selections reuse the in-session result. Evidence and before/after corner plots are in `.local/tum-worker/`; production was not updated.

The broader compact-data migration and its limited 15-layout sample are documented in [the rollout note](31-width-corridor-rollout.md). Inspect placement is removed from the application. No new imagery is used by the migration.

The Smolensk and Ahvenisto west-side corner regressions now check inward apex placement, precise lap-time improvement, all sampled/intermediate car envelopes against paired edges, and preserved source-only GeoJSON. The earlier broad three-venue/sampled rollout results above describe their dated solver versions; the apex correction has its own focused validation in Docs/33-line-calculation-and-apex-fixes.md.
